# Identity proofing: discovery, not approved implementation

The R4 plan remains authoritative. These findings were independently traced by `/root/migration_review` during approval-title review. They are inputs to the next design; no proofing authority or production activation is granted by this record.

- Existing Account anchors are globally unique `users.id`, with `users.org_id` as home Company. Passkeys and refresh families reference them. Preserve Account IDs, home Company, credential IDs/RP IDs, refresh/revocation history and historical actor FKs. Do not move accounts or replace them to implement CompanyActor.
- The existing Person owner is `PgPersonPort`; canonical Person identity is `(org_id,id)` with revisions and employee bindings. Directory creation may use `person_id=employee_id`. These bindings are not attended proof. Keep the local owner and IDs. A separate, narrowly restricted identity owner must establish stable human equivalence and versioned Account links, explicitly mapping existing local Person anchors without a globally readable HR directory.
- Backfilled directory/account/passkey/title associations begin UNVERIFIED. Account signup, organization lead, phone uniqueness, OTP, corporate registration and ordinary passkey enrollment cannot mint verified identity or corporate authority.
- Bootstrap requires a witnessed commissioning record binding actual humans, passkeys and verifier scopes. Never production-seed verified roots. Two-person commissioning does not imply a routine third approver.
- Separate low-risk unverified subject intake from the consequential linkage/grant proposal. An already verified scoped verifier may request the grant after attended evidence and live subject challenge, with one different verified authorized person approving. The subject participates without becoming a third approver. Any alternate trust ceremony requires its own reviewed boundary.
- Necessary evidence includes request/version, lawful minimal matching attributes, attended method/time, live subject challenge, verifier scope/effectivity, independently approved linkage, expiry/dispute/revocation, and separate corporate-authority evidence. No ID images, full ID numbers, recordings, biometric templates or hash surrogates of prohibited identifiers. Append-only Person revision JSON is not a purpose/retention-controlled proofing vault.
- Detect self-proof, self-grant through alias/import/delegation and ambiguous duplicate linkage. Ambiguous linkage stays disputed. Collision responses must not disclose other Companies or Accounts.
- Recovery preserves stable human equivalence, revokes old families/credentials and cannot mint an independent second approver. Merge/split, verifier revocation and material correction advance linkage generations and invalidate unexecuted approvals.
- Governance currently resolves `users.employee_id → employee_person_bindings → person_id`; it does not prove identity. Its high-risk prefix classification covers `company.*`, `hr.*`, `payroll.*`, missing other monetary/custody acts while overclassifying harmless directory work. The semantic floor belongs at registered owner actions; aliases/imports/automation inherit it and workflow exception configuration cannot weaken it.
- Existing governance consume is a reusable atomic single-use transaction point, but kind+target is insufficient. Bind action/version, exact typed payload digest/codec, object/draft revision and policy/authority/linkage generations. Revalidate current proof and authority within the mutation transaction/serialization boundary. JWT `verified=true` or stale stored approval metadata is insufficient.
- Preserve historical approvals and canonical hash versions. Do not derive historical approval digests from current objects. Unexecuted legacy work lacking exact evidence requires new review.
- Existing provisioning has cross-Company SECURITY DEFINER helpers and broad topology/default grants. Do not copy that access shape or expose proof-table INSERT/UPDATE to ordinary `console_rt`. Use restricted owner operations/roles, explicit ACL reconciliation, and install/upgrade/topology-reapply negative tests.

The four-owner title correction is necessary prevention, but distinct Accounts still do not prove distinct natural people. Full verified linkage, semantic classification, exact approval binding and execution serialization remain required before R4 acceptance.

## Authentication ownership transition findings

Independent read-only tracing found atomic closures that a second pool alone cannot preserve: auth REST registration combines passkey insertion and bootstrap consumption; provisioning signup/roster/Company/Group creation combines identity facts and initial OTP hashes; identity deactivation combines inactive state, credential/family sweep, session generation and audit; reset/handoff combines codes/revocation/audit; Company removal deletes auth state within the existing owner transaction. Device-login handoffs are an additional credential authority surface.

An ordinary-runtime-callable delete-all-keys helper is not safely monotonic by itself: existing zero-key bootstrap paths permit re-enrollment after deletion/reactivation. A safe ownership transition requires protected persistent ever-enrolled/recovery-required state and a monotonic credential/recovery epoch, or moving the entire existing command into the restricted boundary. Never expose issue/reset/force flags to ordinary runtime SQL. Proposed role/function shapes remain unapproved; implement no role grant from this discovery alone.

The next bounded correction is specified separately in `webauthn-contention.md`: database-clock expiry after actual lock waits and standard verifier serialization on each current credential. It does not replace the required ownership/proofing transition.

Additional indirect ownership routes: ordinary `platform_remove_organization(UUID)` is a SECURITY DEFINER deletion path; forced removal has a distinct command credential. User/Company FK cascades can delete auth state despite direct table REVOKE. Any claimed ownership census must include these paths and protect recovery/tombstone state from delete/recreate bypass. Future global human equivalence/proof records must not blindly cascade with a Company-local Account/directory deletion.

## Next coordinated authentication lock boundary (after accepted0226)

Accepted0226 protects credential UPDATE identity only (`credential-identity-candidate.json`, SHA3f4b0fe0c004f1eee9380b085038fdc36906cf2c820d19d03847cad4026280fc; backend263d1106dba6185f198b78591d5260fb418444fa06f3d4c91f55955e4b894182).39real integration tests and662migration checks pass. No subsequent production source/test change has been made.

Read-only root/reviewer tracing found that changing WebAuthn finish paths alone to Account-first FOR UPDATE can introduce cycles. The approved ownership design requires a coordinated transition, including implicit FK/audit locks:

- `platform/auth/src/webauthn.rs`: registration currently locks ceremony→Account→key; all three assertion paths lock ceremony→key with no Account lock. Expiry is correctly rechecked with DB wall clock after waits and must remain so. Registration's only production borrowed-transaction caller is `platform/auth-rest/src/lib.rs:925`; it invokes the auth owner before bootstrap consumption (no prior explicit row lock).
- `platform/provisioning/src/lib.rs:330`: reset deletes keys first; `issue_bootstrap_if_needed_tx` begins by updating bootstrap rows and later inserts a bootstrap row whose users FK requires KEY SHARE. A new login holding Account FOR UPDATE while waiting for that key can deadlock with reset. Issuance/self-handoff, roster/group/onboarding and cold-admin paths all need caller inventory before adding an Account lock inside that helper.
- `platform/auth-rest/src/lib.rs:1470` and `identity/rest/src/lib.rs:4004`: both self-key deletions independently count→delete→audit, without an Account serialization lock. The last-key floor races; audit actor FKs can acquire Account KEY SHARE after the key lock. Converge through the existing auth owner rather than duplicate fixes. Exact purpose-bound removal step-up remains part of the full design, not supplied by this concurrency fix alone.
- `platform/auth/src/refresh.rs:191`: refresh locks token/family, then inserts replacement token and audit with users FKs. Account-lock changes must include this reverse dependency and preserve after-wait expiry, replay and reuse protections.
- `identity/adapter-postgres/src/lib.rs:512`: deactivation's active UPDATE locks Account, but the already-inactive branch performs an unlocked existence read before credential sweep. Full lifecycle trigger/receipt convergence remains pending.
- Ordinary removal's current definition is migration0080: auth families/keys/ceremonies are deleted before users. Force removal's current outer definition is0196: it calls0208's dynamic public child sweep (including auth children) before UPDATEusers/DELETEusers. Both must acquire sorted Account locks before child work; preserve all historical SQL bytes through additive migration. Existing Company-removal authority/history gaps are not resolved by lock ordering.
- Legacy migration0036's cold-admin rehome deletes/reinserts users; inspect this additional lifecycle path. Do not silently relabel Account/RP identity or retire then resurrect UUIDs.

Reviewer `/root/migration_review` confirmed the above lock dependency finding. FOR NO KEY UPDATE is a candidate serialization mode: it conflicts with lifecycle UPDATE/DELETE while allowing child FK KEY SHARE. It does not repair explicit late Account UPDATE/DELETE or a caller already holding a ceremony/key. Lock strength, all supported producers/consumers and deterministic observed-wait tests in both schedules must be settled before implementation. This is discovery, not an approved new test set, completed lock-order transition or permission to expose partial authentication.
